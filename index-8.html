Everything starts
with a dream.

A teenager in Croatia looked at an old car and saw something nobody else could see yet. He didn’t ask for permission. He started building.

  1. The beginning

    A curious kid who built things

    Born in 1988 in Livno, Mate Rimac grew up in Croatia — and as a teenager won awards at international innovation competitions with his own inventions.

  2. The garage

    An old BMW, rebuilt as electric

    He converted a petrol BMW 3 Series into an electric car, largely by hand. The car went on to set world records for electric-vehicle acceleration.

  3. 2009

    Rimac Automobili is founded

    A tiny company near Zagreb with one audacious goal: build the world’s best electric hypercar, from Croatia.

  4. 2011

    Concept_One

    Rimac’s first electric hypercar is unveiled at the Frankfurt Motor Show. The world starts paying attention.

  5. 2021

    Bugatti Rimac

    The young Croatian company and one of the most storied names in automotive history join forces, with Mate Rimac as CEO.

  6. Today

    Nevera, and the proof

    An electric hypercar designed, engineered and handcrafted in Croatia — and a reminder that the distance between a garage and the top of the world is a decision.

A Dreamers Club in every town.And in every village.

Every future Rimac was once a kid with a pencil and too big an idea. Dreamers Clubs are where those ideas get a room, a mentor and an audience.

Like an after-school circle — for big stuff

A weekly meeting in a school, library, makerspace or community hall. Kids and young people aged 8 to 18 dream out loud, sketch, build and present.

Run by locals, backed by a global team

A teacher, an engineer, a parent or an older student hosts the club. The Impossible Team would provide the method, challenges and mentors.

From village hall to world stage

The boldest ideas from local clubs could be invited to regional showcases and, one day, to work alongside the Impossible Team itself.

How a club meeting works

Ninety minutes. Four moves.

  1. Dream

    Everyone answers one question: what would you build if nothing could stop you?

  2. Draw

    Ideas go on paper — sketches, diagrams, stories. Bad drawings encouraged.

  3. Build

    Cardboard, code, LEGO, electronics. A first rough prototype of the impossible.

  4. Show

    Each dreamer presents to the room. The rule: questions, never “that’s impossible.”

One Impossible Team.

Talent is everywhere. Access isn’t. The Impossible Team is designed to close that gap.

  • Expertise and talent

    Engineers, scientists, designers and founders who have built hard things before.

  • Strategy and entrepreneurship

    Help turning a brilliant idea into a plan, a team and a company.

  • Technology and engineering

    Access to tools, labs and engineering know-how for prototypes that actually work.

  • Creative and business development

    Brand, story and go-to-market, so the world understands what you’ve made.

  • Networks and partners

    Introductions to the people and organisations that can open the next door.

  • Investment and acceleration

    For the boldest projects, potential investment to move faster.

These are proposed capabilities of a concept initiative, not services currently offered.
